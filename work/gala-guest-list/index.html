01What it is

A nonprofit ran its annual gala across four disconnected sources: ticket sales, sponsorship pledges paid by check or bank transfer, comp tickets tracked by individual staff, and a shared spreadsheet. Sponsors emailed guest names that staff retyped, badges were mail-merged by hand, and the door ran on a printed list.

02How it works

Three apps over one Postgres database, built by a team of four.

  • Staff app: gala setup, sponsorship levels, a ticketing-platform import, orders with a payment ledger, tables, badges, a staff-approved email queue, CSV reports and a change log.
  • Sponsor portal: a private link per order, in four languages, where a sponsor names and edits their guests until the deadline. No account needed.
  • iPad check-in: A-to-Z search, QR e-ticket scanning, walk-ins, and check-in that keeps working when the Wi-Fi drops and syncs when it returns.

03The hard part

My part was the backend, and most of it is about who can do what.

  • Row-level security in Postgres for three staff roles, a sign-in hook that admits only the staff list, and 6-digit email codes instead of passwords.
  • Sponsor links and QR e-tickets on random tokens of at least 128 bits, stored only as SHA-256 hashes, scoped to one order or one seat, and expiring at the deadline or after gala night.
  • An append-only change log of every edit (who, when, before and after) that only gala admins can read, and offline sync where the first arrival wins when two iPads check in the same guest.
  • When the team ran out of GitHub Actions minutes, a local CI fast lane: a full seven-stage run marks the commit on GitHub, so every PR still gets the complete check.

04Bugs the testing caught

Click-through tests of every button on a local copy found real defects.

  • A QR check-in that admitted the guest without saying who had come in.
  • Relationship managers receiving the whole change log, which only gala admins should read.
  • A payment form that let the same check, or an extra zero, through (from a teammate's review).

05What it doesn't do

A working prototype, not production.

  • Every record in testing and in the walkthrough is invented. Production hosting and real data are the client's decision after Demo Day.
  • The repository is private and the apps sit behind a sign-in gate, so there is no public link. Numbers are as of October 2026.
