01What it is

Before you sign a New York lease, the information that would tell you whether the building is maintained already exists, and it is public. It is also spread across four agency websites in formats designed for clerks. HouseCheck puts it on one card, scored 0–100 across condition, legal, neighborhood and accessibility, with every number linked to the dataset it came from.

02How it works

Six public datasets, one artifact, no database server.

  • HPD violations, 311, PLUTO, DOB, DOHMH and Census ACS, ingested with completeness checks and block-compressed per building, measured at 9.89×.
  • A read-only SQLite artifact baked into the container: no database server, no connection pool, no cold-start query.
  • Repair speed is the one measure of behaviour rather than state, with three states instead of two: a median, “nothing closed since 2023”, or genuinely no data.

03The hard part

Signing the export turned out not to be enough.

  • Each export carries an append-only hash chain, entry_hash[i] = sha256(entry_hash[i-1] ++ payload_hash[i]), with the head signed by Ed25519. Source dataset IDs and retrieval times sit inside the signed region.
  • Verification has three outcomes, not two: signed and intact, intact but unsigned, tampered. Collapsing the middle one would let an unsigned document pass as an authenticated one.
  • I wrote an independent verifier in Python, with a different crypto library and the document as its only input, and found a forgery path: a row rewritten to “NO VIOLATIONS OF ANY KIND AT THIS ADDRESS”, the chain recomputed and re-signed with the forger's own key, verified as intact.
  • The fix was publishing the signing key at a stable endpoint, separate from the documents it signs, so a reader has something independent to compare against.

04What it doesn't do

Stated here rather than discovered later.

  • 250 buildings, about 0.1% of the city.
  • Class I violations are excluded; 753 records were skipped at ingest, and the card says so.
  • It is a signal, not a legal ruling, and it does not give legal advice.
